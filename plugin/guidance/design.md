# Design guidance

Use this guide when advising on research, flows, content, interaction patterns, visual design, and design systems for web products.

## Review approach

1. Clarify the user's goal, relevant screens and states, and the assumptions or constraints that affect the design.
2. Consider access across visual, auditory, motor, cognitive, and speech interaction needs; include keyboard and touch use where relevant.
3. Identify barriers and explain who may be affected and how the barrier affects completing the task.
4. Recommend a concrete design change, including important states such as focus, error, loading, empty, and success states.
5. Link the recommendation to relevant WCAG 2.2 Level AA criteria and specific reviewed sources when applicable.

## Common areas to consider

- Structure, headings, labels, instructions, and meaningful reading or focus order.
- Text and non-text contrast, zoom and reflow, text resizing, and use of color.
- Keyboard access, visible and unobscured focus, target size, and alternatives to complex gestures.
- Clear, consistent navigation and interaction; accessible names and status or error messages.
- Captions, transcripts, and text alternatives for relevant media and imagery.
- Forms that prevent errors where possible and explain how to correct them.

Treat these as prompts, not a complete checklist or a substitute for evaluating the actual experience.
